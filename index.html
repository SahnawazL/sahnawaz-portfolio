Productivity

Excel Techniques That Actually Save Hours

tap to read ↓

XLOOKUP instead of nested VLOOKUPs, INDEX-MATCH for two-way lookups, pivot tables with calculated fields instead of a wall of manual SUMIFs — the difference between a report that takes twenty minutes and one that takes twenty seconds, learned handling catalog and ops data at Flipkart and Xiaomi.

Design

Why Responsive Design Matters

tap to read ↓

Fluid type with clamp() instead of a pile of fixed breakpoints, mobile-first media queries, and testing at 375px before ever touching 1440px — because Google's mobile-first indexing means a layout that breaks on a phone quietly hurts your desktop ranking too.

Engineering

Why I Ditched Exact-Version Dependency Matching

tap to read ↓

My first pass at the Dependency Freshness score (part of the "Recently Shipped" panel below) flagged anything that wasn't npm's literal newest release — so a healthy project one patch behind looked neglected. Switched to comparing major versions instead: React 17 vs 18 is a real gap, 18.2 vs 18.3 isn't.

Performance

Optimizing Frontend for Speed

tap to read ↓

Halving the particle-field count on mobile (25 down to 12), turning off AOS scroll animations below the mobile breakpoint, and deferring non-critical scripts — the unglamorous trims on this exact page that actually move a Lighthouse score, unlike a bigger hero image.

Mindset

What Handling Angry Customers Taught Me About Debugging

tap to read ↓

Escalation calls at Flipkart and Xiaomi drilled in one habit: the person in front of you isn't the problem, the gap behind the scenes is. Slow down, ask what actually broke instead of guessing, fix the real cause — the exact same order of operations I now follow every time something misbehaves in code instead of on a call.

Clarity

Writing for People Who've Never Opened a Government Form

tap to read ↓

Welfare scheme pages are written for officials, not for the people who actually qualify. Building YojanaSahay meant turning dense eligibility rules into a sentence someone can act on the first time they read it — the same standard I held myself to training new agents at Rapido: if it needs a follow-up question, it wasn't explained well enough.

Ownership

Why I'd Rather Ship Late Than Let Something Break Quietly

tap to read ↓

Running a platform solo means there's no one behind me to catch what I miss, so I'd rather push a feature a week later than have it fail silently on someone counting on it. It's the same line I held on live support floors at Xiaomi and Flipkart — get it right before you close it out, not after someone comes back asking why it didn't work.
